## Project title

Cell-type-resolved regulatory genetics of systemic sclerosis at IL12RB2

## Abstract (≤500 words)

Systemic sclerosis (SSc) is an immune-mediated fibrotic disease with substantial heterogeneity in genetic susceptibility. We are integrating publicly released SSc genome-wide association summary statistics with TenK10K Phase 1 single-cell expression quantitative trait locus (sc-eQTL) summary statistics to test whether disease association signals at the IL12RB2 locus share one or more causal genetic signals with cell-type-resolved regulation of IL12RB2 expression. The analysis is restricted to the published IL12RB2 ±100 kb cis window and to pre-specified immune cell types (NK, CD8_TEM, CD4_CTL and CD4_TCM).

Public TenK10K summary statistics demonstrate strong IL12RB2 eQTL signals but LD-aware multi-signal inference requires a genotype correlation matrix matched to the source cohort. We therefore request access to TenK10K Phase 1 genotype data (`EGAS50000001654 / EGAD50000002377`) only if the already-derived per-gene IL12RB2 LD matrix cannot be provided directly by the data producers. The controlled data would be used solely to reconstruct the signed genotype correlation matrix for the IL12RB2 ±100 kb region and to validate variant order and allele coding against public TenK10K summary statistics. No participant-level phenotype modelling, re-identification, ancestry inference, clinical diagnosis, commercial development, or individual-level linkage to external datasets is planned.

All controlled-access analyses will be performed in an institutionally approved secure computing environment by named authorised personnel. Controlled genotype data and sensitive derivatives will not be placed in public repositories or shared with unauthorised collaborators. Publications will report only aggregate statistical results and will acknowledge/cite the TenK10K data producers and EGA accessions. Controlled data will be destroyed or otherwise handled at project completion in accordance with the TenK10K Data Access Agreement and institutional requirements.

The scientific objective is to distinguish shared from distinct SSc–IL12RB2 genetic signals using reproducible multi-signal fine-mapping/colocalisation, while explicitly retaining negative or inconclusive results.
